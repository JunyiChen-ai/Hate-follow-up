We thank the reviewer for recognizing the importance of the annotation-cost problem, the venue relevance, and the breadth of the evaluation. We respond to the three weaknesses with new controlled experiments and verified literature.

**W1: Methodological novelty — coarse-to-fine composition of existing MLLMs is a common paradigm.**

We agree that coarse-to-fine cascades are a familiar shape, and we do not rest the contribution on the pipeline shape. The contribution is what fills the shape *without labels*, and each piece is tested by a counterfactual ablation rather than asserted. (a) The routing signal: prior label-free hate detectors decide from generated text; TRIAGE derives a calibrated posterior from the unlabeled pool's own score distribution, which is what makes error-concentrated routing possible with no supervision — as reported in Table 2 of the paper, removing this calibration costs 6.3 average accuracy, and replacing the selected region with a random subset of identical size costs 2.4, so the region is doing measurable work beyond budget-saving. (b) The resolution rule: sequential Bayesian updating over *heterogeneous* verifiers with label-free early stopping, not repeated sampling — replacing it with self-consistency over the single strongest verifier costs 2.9, and fixed three-verifier majority voting costs 1.3 (Table 2). A generic cascade specifies neither mechanism. We will sharpen this positioning against multi-LLM routing/cascade systems in the related-work section.

**W2: Missing baselines — post-training LLM methods and SAGE (ACL 2026).**

We thank the reviewer for the SAGE pointer. SAGE (Huang et al., ACL 2026) is a *supervised* mixture-of-experts detector (RoBERTa/MFCC/VideoMAE experts trained on each target dataset), so it belongs in the supervised group of Table 1 rather than the label-free group; it reports HateMM 87.1 ACC under a random 7:1:2 split and does not evaluate ImpliHateVid. We reproduced SAGE on our exact fixed splits with the authors' released code and hyperparameters, extending it to ImpliHateVid as well: it reaches **79.5 / 72.7 / 67.8 / 85.8 ACC on HateMM / MHClip-EN / MHClip-ZH / ImpliHateVid, versus TRIAGE's 86.0 / 79.5 / 83.9 / 82.8 with zero labels** — label-free TRIAGE leads on three of the four under the common protocol, while SAGE stays ahead on ImpliHateVid, the benchmark with the largest balanced labeled training set. The drop from SAGE's published HateMM/MHClip numbers is consistent with the split sensitivity its own paper documents (their HCC1 re-run: 78.3 under their split vs HCC1's reported 85.4 on its fixed split); reproduction notes: our fixed validation splits, our common ASR transcripts instead of Whisper-turbo, single seed. The revision will include SAGE (published and reproduced, with protocol notes) in the supervised group. On post-training methods: the representative systems (MuPHI, reward-optimized multimodal harm detection; ExPO-HM, ICLR 2026; RA-HMD, EMNLP 2025) are image–text (meme) methods whose post-training consumes labeled hate data — the supervision our setting removes — and none reports hateful-video results; the training-free video member of this family, MARS, is already in Table 1. We will add all of these to the related work.

**W3: Why does label-free outperform some supervised methods — is the gain just strong backbone MLLMs?**

We can now answer this with a direct control. If the backbone were the explanation, running our strongest backbone uniformly as a single-pass classifier (same evaluation protocol as Table 1) should match TRIAGE. It does not. Under the same policy prompt our verifiers use (ACC, all four datasets):

| Method | HateMM | MHClip-EN | MHClip-ZH | ImpliHateVid | Avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Largest backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) on every video | 79.9 | 74.5 | 80.5 | 69.1 | 76.0 |
| Best uniform single backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) on every video | 85.6 | 75.2 | 83.2 | 73.4 | 79.4 |
| TRIAGE (same backbones, routed) | 86.0* | 79.5 | 83.9 | 82.8 | 83.1 |

(*86.0 versus 86.5 in Table 1: a post-submission reproducibility audit found that one boundary-region verifier verdict differs in the current verifier outputs, so we re-pinned all reported numbers to the exactly reproducible artifacts; Table 1 will be corrected in the revision; no conclusion changes.) Every individual backbone, applied uniformly, is below TRIAGE on every dataset. Scale does not even order the backbones: under uniform application the 32B model outperforms the 72B model on all four datasets, so "use a bigger model everywhere" is not the mechanism behind our results. Two paper ablations complete the attribution (Table 2): replacing routed heterogeneous arbitration with self-consistency over the same strong backbone costs 2.9 average accuracy, and dropping arbitration entirely costs 4.2. So the gain is attributable to the label-free routing and arbitration structure, not to model scale.

As to why label-free can rival supervision at all: the supervised baselines train on each benchmark's labeled split (roughly 550 to 1,300 videos), so their ceiling is set by annotation scale, while MLLMs bring pretraining-scale knowledge of hate targets, slurs, and policy language; TRIAGE's contribution is converting that knowledge into calibrated, selectively-spent decisions. The reproduced SAGE shows this directly on MHClip-ZH: all three of its supervised experts saturate at ~0.68 accuracy (text 0.678, audio 0.671, vision 0.678 — none dead or dominant), so the binding constraint is the culture-specific knowledge learnable from a few hundred labeled Chinese videos, which TRIAGE's verifiers inherit from pretraining instead. We calibrate the claim honestly: supervised systems remain ahead where in-domain data is plentiful (the ImpliHateVid framework reports 87.5 versus our 82.8 on its own benchmark), and our claim is competitiveness at zero annotation cost, not that supervision is obsolete. We will add this analysis to the revision.
