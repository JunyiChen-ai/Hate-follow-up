## Why this does not reduce to pure calibration

For fixed selected depth and one token per class, the contrastive margin equals the mature margin minus the premature margin. That algebra alone does not make it calibration of the mature prediction: two windows with the same mature margin can have different premature margins and therefore different contrastive scores. Dynamic selection additionally uses full-vocabulary intermediate distributions. With multiple token variants per class, the specified token-level contrast followed by class aggregation generally is not even the difference of the two class margins.

The distinction rests on the source of information, not on whether subtraction occurs after the final transformer block. Applying only an offset, temperature or fitted remapping to the old output would fall on the other side of this boundary. Here intermediate states must actually enter the final scoring path, as the code review should verify. Reading internal states does not itself prove that the resulting signal represents hateful evidence, or that any gain is more than a change in confidence/ranking; those are experimental questions.

## Primary source verification

[DoLa: Decoding by Contrasting Layers Improves Factuality in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03883), §2–3, projects intermediate states through the vocabulary head, selects a premature layer by divergence from the final distribution, and contrasts log probabilities. It already evaluates supplied multiple-choice continuations as well as generated text. Therefore using a finite answer score instead of open-ended generation is not a new source mechanism. The paper chooses candidate-layer buckets using validation and uses an adaptive plausibility constraint, including its multiple-choice setting. Contraster instead fixes all declared even post-block depths and removes that constraint. It does not inherit the source's tuning regime, accuracy guarantees or measured overhead.

The [author's `dola.py`](https://github.com/voidism/DoLa/blob/main/dola.py) was read through its raw source. In `lm_score`, the calls around lines 192–194 use `kl_div(log p, M)` and `kl_div(log q, M)`, where `M=(p+q)/2`. With the function's argument convention, this computes reverse-oriented terms, proportional to `KL(M||p)+KL(M||q)`, rather than standard `KL(p||M)+KL(q||M)`. The vocabulary mean contributes a fixed scale for selection; reversing KL arguments is the consequential difference. The declaration correctly chooses standard JS explicitly and does not claim exact reproduction of that implementation. The same function applies a relative-top plausibility mask to supplied-continuation scores; its omission here is a real adaptation, not something already absent from source classification use.

[LayerCD: Mitigating Hallucination in Multimodal LLMs with Layer Contrastive Decoding](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25177v1), §2, contrasts model responses conditioned on shallow versus deep vision-encoder features. It is relevant multimodal prior art, evaluated on image hallucination tasks such as POPE/MME. It differs from reading several language-decoder residual states in one unchanged forward. Neither first layer contrast nor first multimodal contrastive decoding is an available claim.

## Actual target-task search and distinctions

Searches on 2026-10-03 combined `hateful video`, `hate video`, `video hate speech`, `HateMM`, `HateClipSeg`, and `video safety` with `DoLa`, `decoding by contrasting layers`, `contrastive decoding`, `layer contrast`, `layer-wise`, `premature layer`, `logit`, and `logit lens`. Broader hate-content searches checked whether a target application was hidden under different terminology. Unrelated products/authors named Dola, bibliography co-occurrences, and ordinary contrastive training were not counted as applications.

- [MARS](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15115), §2.2, generates objective, hate-assumed and non-hate-assumed interpretations, then synthesizes them. Its contrasting hypotheses are separate prompted reasoning paths, not mature/premature token distributions from one forward.
- [RAMF](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02743v1), §3.1–3.5, generates adversarial reasoning text and fuses its encoded representations with multimodal features. A search hit co-occurring with reverse-prompt contrastive decoding was not evidence of internal layer contrast. The actual inspected method is structured prompting and learned fusion.
- [IARE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11953v1), §4–5, uses information augmentation, supervised instruction training and DPO on correct/incorrect reasoning paths. Its probability ratios concern preference training; its described inference/baselines do not establish DoLa-style layer readout.
- [CLARA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15905v1) and [MultiHateLoc](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10408v1) were previously read during this agent's Factorizer review. Their local/global or cross-modal contrastive objectives and fusion mechanisms do not establish inference-time subtraction of intermediate and final vocabulary distributions. These prior primary readings were reused, not represented as new full-text reads this round.
- [Risk Awareness Injection](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03402), §2–3, was followed from a layer-wise safety search hit. It analyzes layer-wise visual alignment with unsafe concepts and injects risk vectors into initial visual representations for jailbreak defense. It is a relevant internal-safety intervention, but not the proposed readout and not evidence of layer-contrastive hateful-video detection. Its layer analysis cannot supply Contraster's proposed early-topic/late-hateful-act interpretation.

The [WWW 2026 explicit-evidence-attribution paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3774905.3796488) remains an access boundary: its publisher-indexed abstract was recovered again, but full text was not available in the preceding access attempts. The abstract describes extracted evidence, deterministic ranking and optional reasoning, and does not establish intermediate-layer contrast. It also cannot rule out an unobserved implementation detail. The finding is therefore “no verified target application found,” not exhaustive proof of absence.

## Necessary precision and falsifiable observations

These are source/measurement requirements, not speculative feasibility STOP arguments.

1. **Respect the declared computation.** Post-block indices `2,4,...,34` mean 17 candidate residual states, not embeddings or pre-block states. Apply the native final RMSNorm once to each intermediate state and use the actual final normalized state for the mature read. Verify native mature-margin parity and removal of hooks. The candidate must neither change the prefix/cache nor accidentally read the generated answer token instead of the final query token.

2. **Test standard JS and score equivalence.** In addition to symmetry, zero divergence for identical distributions and the natural-log bound, test an asymmetric distribution pair that distinguishes the declared JS from the author's reversed-KL expression. Use stable log-domain arithmetic. The final branch margin is `logsumexp(a_L-a_l)` over Yes variants minus the corresponding No expression. The common vocabulary log-normalizers cancel; class aggregation must occur after token differences. Do not silently replace this with a difference of native class margins.

3. **Keep the APC omission visible.** Finite binary output does not remove the source paper's concern that tokens with very low probabilities can receive large ratios. A rare capitalization/spacing variant can dominate a class aggregate after contrast. Record the dominant variants and their mature/premature log probabilities, together with the native scores. Such domination is a diagnostic finding, not automatically proof of useful semantic information or a reason to reject the candidate before running it.

4. **Save enough information for the declared controls.** The parent accepted a pre-implementation logging clarification: store every candidate layer's native Yes/No token logits, mature logits, each full-vocabulary log-normalizer, all JS values and the selected layer. Full-vocabulary tensors need not be retained. These small records support the fixed `L/2` control, the selected-premature-logit window permutation and token-variant diagnostics without another teacher pass. Record source-window indices and actual changed pairs for the permutation; one-window or unchanged pairs do not test correspondence.

5. **Distinguish the claims tested by each control.** Mature-only tests the contrastive readout as a whole. Fixed middle depth tests the declared dynamic selection relative to that fixed alternative, not an exhaustive claim of optimal layer choice. Window permutation tests dependence on matched mature/premature reads while preserving their marginal availability. Common-shift and raw-versus-r6 comparisons distinguish raw temporal reordering from downstream score effects. Every separately claimed novel component still needs rule 14g support on both corpora. Selected-layer histograms or large JS are not evidence that early layers encode topic while later layers encode hateful acts; the declared group-mention analysis and gain/loss cases are required before making that stronger interpretation.

6. **Count the extra projection work.** Outer transformer calls remain `3+B`, but each branch requires full-vocabulary mature and 17 intermediate projections, versus the native limited label rows. The FP32 head copy and projection bandwidth are real costs. Report actual smoke timing, peak memory and per-corpus native/contrastive deployment totals. Chunking the head for memory may preserve the declared computation; choosing a cheaper subset of layers after observing performance would be a new configuration requiring declaration.

No completed exact Contraster experiment was supplied in this review's scope. The parent reports that earlier attribution probes failed, but those results were not read or used here, and they do not by themselves test a depth trajectory. Keep the method/coefficients fixed for the full paired run and evaluate all three metrics on both corpora. A supported pending-run outcome takes priority; this PASS does not require launching another candidate after the user's objective has already been supported.
